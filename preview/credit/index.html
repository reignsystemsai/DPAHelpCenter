Start with your current employer.

Based on your entries. This is preparation progress, not verified employment or a lending decision.

Changed employers?

A two-year history can span several employers. Lenders look for consistent, predictable income and may review job or industry changes. Two years at the same company is not always required.

A shorter gap?

A break under six months is not automatically acceptable. Explain it and show your current employment and income records. Program rules and lender review still apply.

Six months or longer?

Longer breaks can raise questions about stable, continuing income. FHA generally requires at least six months in the current job and a two-year work history before the extended absence. Other programs can differ.

School, military service, and other documented circumstances may help explain your history. Your lender decides what qualifies and may request older records.

Build your self-employment history.

Record when you began your business or contract work, including earlier businesses and any breaks. Generally prepare two years of history and filed tax returns with the applicable schedules.

Moved from W-2 to 1099?

Your earlier W-2 work stays on your timeline, but does not automatically count as self-employment. Some programs allow a shorter self-employment history with documented related experience and a full year of self-employment income on filed returns. Your lender reviews the details.

Questions about write-offs?

Use Income & DTI to review income amounts and how business deductions can affect qualifying income. Keep this section focused on dates, continuity, and records.
