Employment Readiness

Your work history can decide if you’re DPA ready.

Most lenders want to see a stable 2-year history of work, school, military, or income. You do not always need to be at the same job for two full years.
✓ Same job can help
✓ Job changes may be okay
✓ College can count
✓ Gaps may need explaining
Common Lender Review
2YR
A clear 2-year work or school history can help make your DPA file stronger.

Why employment matters

Down payment assistance still requires mortgage approval. Your income must look stable, documentable, and likely to continue.

Stable Income

Lenders want to know your income can support the future mortgage payment.

2-Year History

Work, school, military, or related career training may help support your file.

Income Type

W2, hourly, salary, bonus, commission, 1099, and self-employed income may be reviewed differently.

What usually works

A job history does not have to be perfect. It needs to make sense, show stability, and be easy for the lender to document.

Strong Path

Employed for 2 years

If you have been steadily employed for two years, your employment history may be easier to approve.

✓ Same job for two years is strong
✓ Same career field can also help
✓ Paystubs and W2s may be needed
Still Possible

Changed jobs recently

Switching jobs does not automatically disqualify you if your work history still makes sense.

✓ Job changes can be acceptable
✓ Higher income can help
✓ Gaps may need an explanation
Special Case

Graduated college

College or training may count if your new job connects to your degree, license, or field of study.

✓ School history may count
✓ Degree or training can support the file
✓ Offer letter or paystubs may be needed
Needs Review

In and out of work

Major employment gaps, short jobs, or unstable income may need a deeper review before applying.

✓ Explain gaps clearly
✓ Gather paystubs and W2s
✓ Avoid guessing before lender review
Important: Employment history does not have to be perfect, but it must make sense to the lender.

1099 or self-employed?

If you are 1099 or self-employed, lenders may need tax returns, bank statements, or profit documentation. Write-offs can reduce qualifying income, so tax review matters.
